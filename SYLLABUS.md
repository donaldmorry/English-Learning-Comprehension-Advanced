# SYLLABUS
## Advanced English: Reading & Vocabulary — 50 Lessons

Each lesson: one original passage of about 1,000 words (range 985–1,215; mean 1,090),
followed by 22 vocabulary items and 6 collocations — 1,400 glossed items across the course.
Level throughout: **C1–C2**.

There are no units and no thematic grouping. The fifty lessons run in a deliberately
irregular order: no two consecutive lessons come from the same field, and no two
consecutive lessons open in the same register. A passage on market microstructure is
followed by one on coral, then one on compilers. This is the course's single most
important structural decision, and the reasoning behind it is given at the end of this
document.

## Design principles behind this plan

**1. Interleaving, not blocking.** Material grouped by subject is more comfortable to
study and demonstrably worse to learn from. Blocked practice lets a reader settle into
one vocabulary field and one register and coast; mixed practice forces the reader to
work out, every single time, what kind of text they are in and what it is trying to do.
The gain shows up on delayed testing rather than during the session, which is why it
feels harder and is not. Lesson 23 makes the argument explicitly and is worth reading
early.

**2. No repeated voice.** Style is assigned before the writing begins, and all fifty
descriptors in the table above are distinct. A learner moving through consecutive
lessons meets, in turn, a consultant's report, a biologist's scrappy notebook and a
persuasive health essay — three different Englishes about three unrelated subjects.

**3. A scientific spine, widely spaced.** Thirty-eight of the fifty lessons sit in
science, engineering, computing, finance or industry, as requested. The remainder —
language, cities, history, forensics, sport, food, conservation, craft — are distributed
throughout rather than collected at the end, so that a learner finishes with vocabulary
for a laboratory *and* for a conversation.

**4. Vocabulary chosen for transfer.** Glossed words are overwhelmingly items that
travel between subjects: *attenuate, provisional, corroborate, incremental, tractable,
salient, proprietary*. Narrow jargon is explained inside the passage itself rather than
being offloaded to the glossary.

**5. One fixed point.** Lesson 50, *The Long Attention*, is the only lesson whose
position is not random. It is written as a closing essay — it addresses the reader who
has finished, and refers back across the course — so it stays last. Everything before it
was shuffled.

**6. Exclusions honoured throughout.** No entertainment or celebrity material, no
partisan or geopolitically contested argument, no adult content, no religion or the
supernatural, and no verse or song.
